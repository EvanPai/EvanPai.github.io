About

A longer introduction

Background

I grew up and studied in Taiwan, and finished a Bachelor of Computer Science at National Tsing Hua University in July 2025, graduating with a 3.97 GPA and ranked 43rd of 183 in my class.

My degree pulled me steadily toward the systems end of machine learning. Between 2023 and 2025 I was a teaching assistant for four NTHU courses — Operating Systems, Parallel Programming, and both the fundamental and advanced High Performance Computing Cluster Practice courses — which meant two years of explaining schedulers, MPI, and GPU clusters to people who had not seen them before. Teaching a thing is still the fastest way I know to find out whether I actually understand it.

Alongside that I interned as a Data Scientist at IBM in Taipei and as a Software Engineer at the National Center for High-performance Computing, where I built a data re-uploading classifier using quantum machine learning methods in PennyLane.

Why data science

Most of my work has been on the engineering side of a model: how fast it runs, how many GPUs it needs, how to squeeze another 3× out of the inference path. That is satisfying, and it is only half the problem.

The other half kept showing up as a question I could not answer properly. A benchmark improves — is the improvement real, or an artifact of how it was measured? A model reports an accuracy — would it hold on next month’s data? During the SC24 Reproducibility Challenge my team reproduced the findings of the DataLife paper on the 1000 Genomes Project, and that experience made the gap obvious: I had strong intuitions about systems and weak ones about evidence.

MDS is me closing that gap deliberately — statistics, inference, and experiment design, taught properly rather than picked up in passing.
